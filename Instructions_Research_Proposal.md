## What to add
Your research proposal should answer in clear prose the following questions:

- *What is your research question?* You should be able to explain it in a few sentences.
  
- *Why should other people care about it?* If you bring great results about a question no one cares about, you will not have a good time publishing your paper. The best type of motivation, even though not available for all topics, comes from cool empirical observations that is either at odds with existing theories, or maybe something new that hadn't shown up in the data before.

- *What exactly is your contribution?* Given what has already been done, what will your paper add to the literature? Be specific. Are you tackling an old problem with new data and new tools? Are you analyzing something weird and new no one has looked at before? 
Make sure to add a literature review section that is A) long enough to tell me how your idea is novel but B) short enough to not take too much space from your actual idea.

- *Is your idea feasible?* Here, let's divide it in two branches. 
  1. If it is an empirical proposal, how would you get the data you need? What are the main difficulties you foresee in getting the data? Is it expensive data? Is it messy? Be as specific as possible.
  2. If it is a theoretical proposal, what are the main frameworks you would use? Do you think you would need to learn a new math tool to tackle it? If it's a large model, do you think solving the model would be too hard? What kind of complication do you foresee?


## Length

- The proposal should have 5 to 10 pages. 
- You should include all relevant figures, tables, and references in that space.
- If you end up doing more progress than you had expected and think you need more than 10 pages, please check with me first. Don't worry, this is a good problem to have.

## How to deliver it
You will email it to me as a PDF file before the deadline. I will send it back to you with comments and with a grade out of 100 points.

## Scope

Even though I plan to cover quite a few topics with you, they will be very foundational. In that sense, not all the concepts and models we will study are "at the research frontier". But you know, before learning how to drive a Porsche around Nürburgring, you first need to learn how to use a Prius to buy some groceries.

In that sense, I expect you to read *way more* than what we will cover in class. **Your research proposal does not need to be strictly related to any of the topics we will cover, even though it has to be within the realms of Asset Pricing, very broadly defined.** Check what's being published in the top journals (like the Journal of Finance, the Journal of Financial Economics, the Review of Financial Studies, Review of Finance, the Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, Management Science,etc) to get a sense of what is being done at the research frontier.

Example 1: It is unlikely we will have time to cover Demand-Based Asset Pricing. But maybe you are taking a class in IO as well and you'd like to read more about this IO-Finance connection. Ok, great, go ahead, try to write something related to it.

Example 2: International Finance is a very large literature on its own. I do not know how much of it we can cover. But there are plenty of interesting things there, with lots of connections to how we think about traders pricing assets denominated in different currencies. If you think this is cool and want to explore it, be my guest.

Example 3: some of you might be an Econometrician in disguise (sounds familiar?). This is not a Financial Econometrics class, but many Asset Pricing papers depend on new econometric tools. If you want to explore this connection and try to come up with a research proposal about it, go ahead.

## Peer-Review

You will be randomly assigned to review one of your classmates' proposals. You will have to provide detailed comments on how to improve the proposal. Essentially, you need to answer:

- What are the strengths of the proposal?
- What are the weaknesses of the proposal?
- What specific suggestions do you have to improve the proposal? It's really easy to just find flaws. But fixing flaws is the hard part. So please be constructive.

You will be graded on the quality of your review. Your review is due one week after the proposal submission deadline. It should have no more than two pages. Please email it to me as a PDF file. This will contribute 15% of your overall grade.
